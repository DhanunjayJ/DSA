### The Greedy Choice
The "Greedy Choice" here is: **Whenever you see a dip ($nums[i] > nums[i+1]$), close that gap immediately using the widest possible subarray.**

Because the problem allows you to pick **any subarray** and add any **positive $x$**, the most "selfish" yet "optimal" move you can make is to pick the subarray from $i+1$ all the way to the end of the array ($n-1$). 

**Why is this the best choice?**
1. **Local Fix:** Adding $nums[i] - nums[i+1]$ to $nums[i+1]$ fixes the immediate violation ($nums[i] \le nums[i+1]$).
2. **Global Benefit:** By increasing *everything* after $i$ by the same amount, you preserve all the relative relationships you already had or will need to fix later. You aren't "hurting" the future of the array because a non-decreasing array only stays non-decreasing if you lift the tail up.

---

### Local Best vs. Global Best
* **Local Best:** "I have a gap of size $D$ between index $i$ and $i+1$. I will add exactly $D$ to $nums[i+1]$ to make it equal to $nums[i]$."
* **Global Best:** By applying this $D$ to the entire suffix (from $i+1$ to $n-1$), you effectively "lift" the rest of the world. This ensures that you never have to pay for that specific gap again.

### Why you don't need a "Prefix Sum" or "Actual Incrementing"
You realized the most important trick: **The operations are additive but independent.**

Imagine the array: `[5, 1, 2, 3]`
1. At `i=0`, `5 > 1`. Gap = $4$. You "lift" `[1, 2, 3]` by $4$. The array *effectively* becomes `[5, 5, 6, 7]`.
2. Now check the new `nums[1]` vs `nums[2]`. Since both were lifted by $4$, their relative difference ($1$ vs $2$) stayed the same! 
3. Because the relative differences in the "tail" never change when you lift the whole tail, you only ever need to care about the **original** drops.

### The "Regret" Test
If you only lifted the single element `nums[i+1]`, you might **regret** it because `nums[i+1]` might now be greater than `nums[i+2]`, creating a *new* problem. By lifting the whole subarray to the end, you ensure you never create a new problem while fixing an old one. That is the definition of a Greedy choice with no regret!

**Final Verdict:** Your logic of summing up all `Math.max(0, nums[i] - nums[i+1])` is the optimal $O(n)$ solution. You've successfully identified that the total $x$ is just the sum of all "descending steps" in the original array.
